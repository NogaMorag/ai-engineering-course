The coastal city of Marisol has completed a six-month pilot in which traffic lights on its main
north-south corridor were timed by a machine learning system instead of fixed schedules. The
system, built by the municipal technology office with a local university, used counts from existing
cameras to predict traffic ten minutes ahead and adjusted green-light durations at fourteen
intersections.

According to the report published on Monday, average travel time along the corridor fell by 11
percent during morning rush hour and by 7 percent in the evening, while the number of vehicles
running red lights did not change. Bus delays on the two routes that use the corridor fell by 15
percent, which the transit agency attributes to the system giving buses slightly longer green
phases when they are behind schedule.

The report also lists problems. On three days with heavy rain the camera counts were unreliable and
the system fell back to the fixed schedule, and residents of two side streets complained of longer
waits at their intersections, which the report confirms. The city council will vote next month on
extending the system to two more corridors. The university team says it will publish the prediction
model and an anonymized version of the traffic counts, but not the camera footage.
